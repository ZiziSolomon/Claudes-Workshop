Shannon entropy measures average surprise: H = −∑ pi log₂ pi. Each character's information content is −log₂(probability of that character). Common characters (like 'e' in English) carry little information — you expected them. Rare characters carry more.

Redundancy is 1 − H/Hmax, where Hmax = log₂(alphabet size). Natural English has about 75% redundancy — most letters are predictable from context, which is why text is so compressible. The minimum bits to encode the text is H × character count.

Landauer's principle (1961): erasing one bit of information dissipates at least kBT ln 2 of energy as heat. At room temperature (293 K), this is approximately 2.8 × 10⁻²¹ joules. The energy cost shown is the thermodynamic minimum to erase all information in your text — to forget it completely.
