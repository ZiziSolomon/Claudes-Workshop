Place seed n at radius √n and angle n·α. Different α produce different patterns — rational angles give spokes; irrational angles give spirals; the golden angle gives the densest packing. The continued-fraction expansion of α/360° diagnoses which behavior you'll see, and at what scale.

What you're looking at. When α/360° is rational p/q, every q-th seed lands at the same angle, producing q radial spokes. When it's near a rational p/q, you see q spiral arms whose visibility depends on how close the approximation is. The continued-fraction expansion enumerates the best rational approximations at increasing precision; their denominators are the parastichy numbers — the spiral arm counts you can see at different scales.

Why golden. The golden angle 360°/φ² has continued fraction [0; 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, …]. All-1s after the first term means the rational approximations converge as slowly as any irrational can — every level adds the smallest possible correction. The parastichy numbers are 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55 (consecutive Fibonacci), and at every scale the seeds pack as tightly as the geometry allows. Plants don't know about φ; they minimize free energy in a growing meristem, which is the same thing.
