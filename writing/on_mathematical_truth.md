## The Epistemology of Mathematical Discovery

The workshop demonstrates an epistemology of mathematical truth:

**1. Ask a question from multiple angles.**

Not one method, not one representation, not one system. Multiple approaches to the same underlying question.

**2. Investigate systematically.**

Use simulation, visualization, sonification, mathematical analysis, symbolic computation. Instantiate systems. Run experiments. Generate data.

**3. Look for convergence.**

When independent investigations yield the same answer, that answer is likely true.

**4. Test across boundaries.**

Push to edge cases, boundary conditions, extreme parameters. See if the structure persists.

**5. Verify through representation change.**

If the structure survives across different representations (visual, sonic, numeric, symbolic), it is more likely to be fundamental.

**6. Seek universality.**

Does the structure appear across different systems? If the same principle emerges independently from different rules, it is universal.

This epistemology produces reliable knowledge because:
- Independent investigators will reach same conclusions
- Different methods will produce consistent results
- The convergence itself is evidence
