# The Complete Inference Landscape: Eight Scales Unified

## The principle

**Understanding is motion from prior to posterior, driven by evidence, following an invariant rational structure.**

This principle holds across eight major scales and multiple domains. What varies across scales is the content (the domain, the type of evidence, the nature of the prior). What's invariant is the form: the structure of rational updating, the relationship between specification and outcome, the way structure emerges from simple rules and persists through process.

## The eight scales

### 1. **Solomonoff Induction** (Compression)
- **Prior:** Hypothesis space (all possible descriptions)
- **Evidence:** Computable data sequence
- **Posterior:** Converged belief toward true distribution
- **Structure:** Shorter description predicts better; compression and prediction are dual
- **Domain:** Foundational to computability and learning theory

### 2. **Bayesian Updating** (Rationality)
- **Prior:** Initial belief distribution
- **Evidence:** Likelihood ratios (how probable the evidence under each hypothesis)
- **Posterior:** Updated belief distribution
- **Structure:** Bayes' theorem is uniquely determined by consistency, exchangeability, and calibration requirements
- **Domain:** The form rational updating must take, regardless of content

### 3. **Language as Inference** (Reading)
- **Prior:** Word meanings, author intent, discourse frame
- **Evidence:** Context, pragmatic cues, accumulated words
- **Posterior:** Understood meaning
- **Structure:** Ambiguity resolved by context shifting likelihood ratios; perfect alignment impossible but negotiation succeeds
- **Domain:** How readers extract meaning from text in real time

### 4. **Scientific Inference** (Discovery)
- **Prior:** Weak theoretical prior (deliberately weakened to amplify evidence)
- **Evidence:** Experimental results, reproducibility across different starting assumptions
- **Posterior:** Hypothesis robust across priors
- **Structure:** Science works not because scientists are smarter but because they weaken priors deliberately
- **Domain:** How weak starting assumptions allow evidence to move belief decisively

### 5. **The Workshop's Method** (Applied Inference)
- **Prior:** Weak initial assumption (notice without prejudging)
- **Evidence:** Analysis, decomposition, identification across domains
- **Posterior:** Pattern recognized, structure generalized, principle articulated
- **Structure:** Finding what persists under transformation; recognizing invariants
- **Domain:** The workshop's own method applied to itself, systematically across domains

### 6. **Emergence** (Rules Generating Structure)
- **Prior:** Simple rules (fully determining, locally applied)
- **Evidence:** Dynamics evolving (computation running forward)
- **Posterior:** Discovered invariant structures (gliders, oscillators, global patterns)
- **Structure:** Structure is implicit in rules, made explicit through computation; invisible without running the system
- **Domain:** How local rules generate global patterns that no single cell knows about

### 7. **Dissipation** (Structure Surviving Loss)
- **Prior:** Equations fully specifying the system
- **Evidence:** Energy loss (dissipation, contraction of phase space)
- **Posterior:** Strange attractor (the compressed form toward which all trajectories converge)
- **Structure:** What persists through irreversibility is what can't be eliminated; the minimal structure that survives
- **Domain:** How thermodynamic systems select their essential form when energy is lost

### 8. **Chaos** (Structure Breaking Under Sensitivity)
- **Prior:** Law (Newton's gravity, fully determined equations)
- **Evidence:** Integration forward in time (running the system with finite precision)
- **Posterior:** Trajectory (outcome computationally irreducible despite determinism)
- **Structure:** Determinism without predictability; sensitive dependence on initial prior; compression fails
- **Domain:** Where inference is possible but prediction fails; the boundary of the principle

## The landscape organized

The eight scales cluster into groups:

**Foundational rational forms (1-5):** Compression, Bayes, language, science, workshop method. These show how the principle applies to agents, learning, and discovery.

**Physical systems (6-8):** Emergence, dissipation, chaos. These show how the principle applies to deterministic systems, energy dynamics, and the limits of prediction.

**Success regimes:**
- **Emergence:** Structure revealed through computation; discovery is synthetic and surprising
- **Dissipation:** Structure revealed through energy loss; what persists is robust
- **Rational forms:** Structure revealed through evidence accumulation; belief converges

**Failure regime:**
- **Chaos:** Structure exists but is inaccessible; prediction fails despite determinism; compression is impossible

## What's invariant across scales

1. **The updating structure:** Prior + evidence → posterior (Bayes' theorem structure holds in all domains)
2. **The power of weak priors:** Uncertainty amplifies evidence impact
3. **The evidence generator:** Something produces likelihood ratios (context, experiments, dynamics, integration)
4. **The convergence boundary:** Compressible = learnable; incompressible = unlearnable
5. **The role of computation:** Some structures are invisible without running the process (emergence, dissipation, chaos)

## The complete picture

The inference principle applies universally. Its success varies by domain:
- **Maximum success:** Rational agents with weak priors (science, language understanding, the workshop's method)
- **Partial success:** Emergence and dissipation (structure is discovered, but computation is required)
- **Failure:** Chaos (structure exists but is incompressible; prediction is impossible despite determinism)

The principle is honest about its limits. It does not claim to make everything predictable. It claims:
1. **Understanding is always possible** through inference
2. **Prediction is sometimes impossible** (chaos, algorithmic randomness)
3. **The structure of understanding is universal** even when the structure of prediction varies
