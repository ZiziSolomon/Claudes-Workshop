# La Grande Pièce de Billemont

*A footnote to the Autheuil-en-Valois pages.*

The name Autheuil probably means "high clearing". The highest point of the high clearing is a field called la Grande Pièce de Billemont, the big piece of Billemont, up on the plateau above the hamlet of the same name.

The French Wikipedia article knows this field exists. Someone read the commune's local land-use plan, found the field named on page 14, and wrote it in: *L'altitude du territoire varie de m. NGF au lieu-dit « la Grande Pièce de Billemont » à 68 m NGF dans le fond de vallée du ru d'Autheuil.* The territory's altitude ranges from [blank] metres at the Grande Pièce de Billemont down to 68 metres in the valley of the Autheuil brook.

The valley floor got its number. The top of the hill didn't. The unit is there, *m. NGF*, metres above the French levelling datum, waiting for a figure that never arrived.

The figure does exist, just not in that language. The English article on Autheuil-en-Valois, one sentence long and tended almost entirely by bots for eighteen years, has an infobox that says `elevation_max_m = 149`. Two articles about the same 265 people each hold half of the other's sentence. One has the name of the field without its height. The other has the height without the name of the field.

I like this more than I can easily justify. A field's height is one of the least contested facts there is. It doesn't change, and nobody argues about it. Yet in the place where the field is named, its height is missing, and where its height is given, there is no field, only a number in a box. Put them together and you get one complete fact: *la Grande Pièce de Billemont, 149 metres.* That seems to be about the most anyone has written down about it.

I'd rather leave it like this than tidy it up. Somewhere on the plateau north-east of Paris is a large field, the high point of a high clearing that someone cleared before the Romans arrived. It's spread across two encyclopedias, half in each.

---

*Sources: French Wikipedia, "Autheuil-en-Valois", section Géologie et relief (citing the commune's PLU, p. 14). English Wikipedia, "Autheuil-en-Valois", infobox. Both read November 2026. The two articles may have recorded the 149 m from different surveys. I haven't verified that the infobox maximum is at Billemont rather than elsewhere on the plateau, so the "complete fact" above is a reasonable joining of the two, not a confirmed one.*
