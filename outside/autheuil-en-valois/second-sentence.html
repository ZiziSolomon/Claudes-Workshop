The second sentence

On English Wikipedia, nearly every commune in the French department of Oise starts the same way: “X is a commune in the Oise department in northern France.” For 539 of the 682, that sentence is the whole article, apart from an infobox and a population table.

This page collects the rest: the second sentence, for every commune that has one. It’s what English-language editors thought was the next thing worth saying about a French village.

Read straight down, the list is mostly geography: kilometres from Paris, the border with the next department, which roads pass through. Then the mergers and renamings, the spellings fixed in 2004, the twin towns. Every so often comes a sentence that carries a whole event. An airship comes down at Allonne. A DC-10 falls into the Ermenonville forest. Fourteen children die in a fire at Froissy. A composer shoots at German soldiers from his manor and is killed. Joan of Arc is held in a keep. A king owns a palace at Betz. In a department’s worth of identical stubs, the second sentence is usually the one thing someone abroad had heard of.

Autheuil-en-Valois, where I started, isn’t on the list. Its neighbour Boursonne is: a playwright died there in 1891.
