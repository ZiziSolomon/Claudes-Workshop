The French Wikipedia article on Autheuil-en-Valois says its name probably means “high clearing”: Latin altus plus Gaulish ialo-, an open space in the forest. It adds, in passing, “comme tous les Autheuils de langue d’oïl”, like all the Autheuils of the langue d’oïl. That clause is the most interesting thing in the article, because it implies the same Gaulish word turned into something else wherever people didn’t speak oïl.

They did. Ialon was one of the commonest place-name elements in Gaul: a clearing, then a farmstead in the clearing, then a village. Two thousand years of sound change wore it down differently in the north and the south. In the north it became -euil: Argenteuil, Nanteuil, Verneuil, Auteuil, Rueil, Arcueil. In the Occitan south it became something like -jol or -jouls. The alder clearing, verno-ialon, is Verneuil in the north and Vernajoul in the Ariège.

Two things stand out. The first is that the department with the most -euil communes in France is Oise, Autheuil’s own, with 22. The forest of Retz and the Valois plateau must once have been a patchwork of clearings. The second is that the blue doesn’t stop where the langue d’oïl stops. It runs on south through Poitou and the Charente and deep into the Dordogne (15 communes, second only to Oise), which is Occitan-speaking country: Mareuil, Excideuil, Limeuil. The orange -uéjouls names sit much further south, packed into the Aveyron, the Lozère and the Gard: Lanuéjouls, Bessuéjouls, Mostuéjouls, Marvejols, Maruéjols-lès-Gardon. Between them is a wide band where the northern spelling won even though the language was southern.

So the clause in the Wikipedia article, “like all the Autheuils of the langue d’oïl”, is slightly too tidy. The Gaulish word didn’t split cleanly along the language border. It split somewhere south of it, along a line that runs closer to the Massif Central than to the Loire, perhaps because spelling, unlike speech, was standardised from the north. (Spelling tells you about scribes as much as sounds. The actual Occitan pronunciation in the Périgord can be quite unlike the French written form.) Brittany, Alsace, the Basque country and most of Gascony are almost empty of both forms, for older and different reasons.

The template didn’t write that clause. Someone did, and it’s the one point in the article where Autheuil-en-Valois connects to anywhere else. Follow it far enough and it ends up in a clearing in the Aveyron.
