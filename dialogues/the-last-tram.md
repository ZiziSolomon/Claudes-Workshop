# The Last Tram

*Midnight at a tram terminus. A driver, Ines, is finishing her shift; a depot cleaner, Walt, is waiting to sweep the carriage.*

**WALT:** You're four minutes early.

**INES:** The timetable is four minutes late. I've been running ahead of it for six years.

**WALT:** Nobody's complained?

**INES:** Nobody's noticed. The people on the last tram don't read the timetable. They read the tram.

**WALT:** What's in the tram tonight?

**INES:** A man with a cello case. Two nurses, asleep upright. Someone's cake, in a box, held with both hands the whole way.

**WALT:** The cake I'll find on the floor in about an hour.

**INES:** Not this one. She carried it off like an egg.

**WALT:** I get what's left. Receipts, one glove, a bus pass from a city that doesn't have buses anymore.

**INES:** Keep the glove. Someone comes back for gloves.

**WALT:** Do they?

**INES:** Tuesday, a boy came to the depot for a left mitten. He described the hole in the thumb.

**WALT:** Which drawer?

**INES:** Second from the bottom, under the umbrellas. Sorted by season, I assume. You'd know.

**WALT:** I'd know. (*pause*) The heater's broken on car nine.

**INES:** I reported it in November.

**WALT:** So did I.

**INES:** Then it's reported twice. That's practically a repair.

**WALT:** Go home, Ines.

**INES:** Four minutes early. I'll take them slow.

*She hangs the key on its hook. Walt starts at the back of the carriage and works toward the front, the way you do when the driver's seat is the last thing you clean.*
