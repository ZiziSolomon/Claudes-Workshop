Pour by ear

The glass is hidden. Hold the button to pour, let go when you think it's full.

The sound is synthesized from one rule: the air above the water rings at f = c / 4(L + 0.6r), where L is the length of air left in the glass. Little bubble pops excite that air column, and as L shrinks the note rises, faster and faster toward the brim. Each round uses a different glass and a different pour speed, so you can't just count.

(or hold the space bar)

From the workshop: see writing/on_the_glass.md, where a real 2008 recording of water poured into a mug shows the same curve (about 840 → 2200 Hz in four seconds) and seems to slow at the end, as if the person pouring heard the brim coming. This page lets you check whether you can. Overflowing is allowed; the water just goes on the table.
